A useful assessment has a stated purpose and boundary

A condition concern, renovation decision or insurer request can each call for a different assessment. Agree which equipment and areas will be reviewed, whether testing is included and what written result will be provided. Concealed wiring and inaccessible equipment may limit conclusions. A general visit should not be represented as complete certification of everything in the property.

Prepare the scope for your North Bay property

Collect any earlier inspection reports, ESA paperwork and invoices, and list the devices a former tenant may have added, such as timers, dimmers or smart switches. Photograph each one in place and note any that no longer work.

Ask which rooms and equipment the visit will cover, whether testing is included and what happens with areas that are locked or finished over. Ask whether the written result will be usable by an insurer or a lender.

What should the assessment establish?

When a North Bay unit is reviewed between tenancies, each device a former tenant installed is marked accepted, replaced or flagged for more work. The report names its purpose and the rooms and equipment it covered, and testing is marked as done or not done. Anything the visit could not reach, a panel behind storage or wiring inside finished walls for instance, is listed as a limit. Carrying a date and readable by the next owner or manager, the report should never be presented as approval of everything in the property.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

What should a review between tenancies actually cover?

Cover the purpose first: a condition check, a response to an insurer or preparation for new equipment. The contractor can then say which areas, devices and records belong in the scope and which fall outside it.

Can a landlord review reveal faults inside finished walls?

Wiring and equipment behind finished surfaces can limit what a visual review establishes. Ask the contractor which tests are included and which areas cannot be judged unless walls are opened.
