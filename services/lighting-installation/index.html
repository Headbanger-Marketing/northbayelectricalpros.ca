Plan the light and the work that makes it possible

A lighting project may involve fixture replacement, new wiring points or a different control layout. The contractor needs to assess existing wiring, mounting support, equipment suitability and access. Fixture appearance alone does not establish compatibility with a dimmer or the suitability of a location. Share product information and the intended room use before installation is priced.

Prepare the scope for your North Bay property

Photograph each existing fixture and the switch that controls it, and write down the shape, mounting style and light source type. Keep the packaging and instruction sheet for each new fixture so the contractor can read them.

Ask whether the existing switch or dimmer suits the new light source, and whether a replacement control is needed. Ask who repairs the ceiling finish if the old fixture left marks or an opening.

What should the assessment establish?

Approval of a like-for-like fixture swap, and its timing, waits on a quote confirming that the existing box and support in the North Bay room suit the new fixture's weight and shape. The quote tells you if the present switch or dimmer works with the new light source and names any control that must change. For lights run by a timer or smart device, the reconnection and testing of that device are described. Every fixture location appears with whatever finish repair is included, and anything not covered is stated plainly.

Ontario electrical safety

ESA says almost all electrical work needs a notification of work. If you hire someone for home electrical work, use a Licensed Electrical Contractor. Agree who files the notification and request the Certificate of Acceptance after ESA review.

Read the current ESA notification and inspection guidance, contractor hiring guidance. You can verify an electrical contractor with ESA before agreeing to work.

Questions before you agree to work

Can I put a dimmer on a light that never had one?

Possibly, but it depends on the fixture, the light source and the wiring at the switch. Send the product details to the contractor and ask for compatibility to be confirmed in the written scope.

Why do some lights flicker or hum on a dimmer?

A mismatch between the dimmer and the light source is one possible reason, though other causes exist. Note which fixtures behave this way, stop using any that smell hot or look discoloured, and let the contractor assess the cause.
