Before work begins

Check what the controls will ask of the wiring.

A new control can need fresh cable and panel access. In a bungalow or split-level with a finished basement, the panel may sit in a finished room or behind storage shelves, and a timer or smart switch may need cable through a closed ceiling.

Write down every control you want, such as dimmers, timers or sensors, and the room each one serves. Then ask the contractor whether the existing wiring and fixtures suit each device before anything is bought.

Does my existing switch wiring limit which smart controls I can use?

It depends on what sits at the switch box and on the fixture being controlled. Describe the existing switch and the lights it runs, then ask the contractor to confirm compatibility in writing before you buy any device.

Can I buy dimmers first and have them installed later?

Buying first can leave you with devices that do not suit your lights. Share the product sheets and the fixture details with the contractor, who can say whether each dimmer and bulb pairing is suitable.

How do I compare quotes that include different controls?

List each control in the same order on every quote request, with its room and purpose. Then check that each written scope names the same devices, the wiring changes and who handles the ESA notification.

Who needs to agree before a finished ceiling is opened up?

The property owner decides, and a condominium corporation or landlord may also need to approve it. Ask the contractor to describe where each cable would run and how any opening would be repaired.
